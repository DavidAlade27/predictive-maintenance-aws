   ## Normal
   Bearing operating within expected parameters. Vibration amplitude (std) stays below 0.15, with no dominant frequency component tied to a specific bearing defect. No action needed; continue routine monitoring.

   ## InnerRace
   A defect on the inner race of the bearing, usually from fatigue spalling or contamination. Produces a distinctive high-frequency vibration signature that rises and falls with shaft rotation, and elevated std readings. Left unaddressed, this typically progresses to full bearing seizure within weeks under continuous load. Recommended action: schedule bearing replacement at next planned downtime; if std continues rising sharply, treat as urgent and inspect within 24-48 hours.

   ## OuterRace
   A defect on the stationary outer race, often from misalignment or excessive radial load. Vibration signature is generally steadier than inner-race faults since the defect location relative to the load zone doesn't rotate. Recommended action: check shaft/housing alignment in addition to replacing the bearing, since outer-race faults often point to an installation or loading issue rather than pure wear.

   ## Ball
   A defect on one or more rolling elements (balls), commonly from lubrication breakdown or particle contamination. Vibration signature shows irregular, non-uniform pulses since faulty balls don't repeat their defect location every rotation in a fixed pattern. Recommended action: check lubrication condition and contamination sources first - this fault type often has a root cause elsewhere in the system, not just a worn part.
